## Why this study fits JFLM

Forensic causality assessment in pregnancy-related trauma — particularly under the Turkish Penal Code Articles 87 and 88 — currently lacks a standardised, reproducible scoring framework. Existing trauma scores (e.g., Pearlman, Kuo) address obstetric outcome prediction but not medico-legal causality attribution. Our work addresses this gap by:

1. Proposing the **TOMEC five-domain weighted score** [0–100] grounded in the Queensland Clinical Guideline (MN19.31, 2019) and contemporary obstetric trauma literature.
2. Retrospectively testing the score on a large corpus of **3,501 Turkish court decisions** (Yargıtay, Danıştay, AYM, AİHM), of which 313 were verified as obstetric-relevant after multi-stage filtering.
3. Reporting **inter-rater reliability (Cohen κ = 0.732, 95% CI 0.523–0.909)** between the developer (single forensic medicine expert) and an independent large language model (GPT-4o, temperature = 0) under double-blind conditions.

## Key methodological transparency

- This is explicitly framed as a **single-expert pilot study** with LLM-assisted exploratory secondary rating; we make no claim of a fully validated multi-rater design.
- All numerical results are **fully reproducible** from committed scripts in the supplementary repository (data SHA-256 fixed, random seeds reported).
- A **prospective multi-expert validation cohort** is planned (Section 10.1) and will form the subject of a separate manuscript.
