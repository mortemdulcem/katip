uygulamak and so scope of liability for consequences of tortious conduct in negligence actions. It is a policy question, the answer to which may well vary
in different fields of human interaction and in different regulatory contexts. If
courts reach for causal language to dress up this central questions it is likely (as
many do) to be, in Aristotelian terms, misdirecting law towards material,
scientific or perceptual questions when the real question is purposive in nature.

As the examples above illustrate, we may have very little difficulty in determining
where these major policy boundaries should lie in a great number of ordinary
cases. These are cases where our moral responsibility beliefs and tort law are in
strong agreement. Few of us would think it morally appropriate to hold the car
driver legally responsible for a lightning strike. In other cases moral responsibilities are difficult. Compare the driver who saves a dying patient on a hospital
delivery by his speed and simultaneously kills another, or the soldier who blows
up his comrades to prevent a traitor giving the enemy information. Law has also
sometimes been clearly wrong in its allocation of responsibility for failure of
interventions to prevent a plaintiff from suffering injuries because of failures in
protective responsibilities. Examples include the archaic and deeply discriminatory traditional rules limiting responsibilities in cases of non-feasance to only a few
special rigid categories such as innkeepers, doctors and guardians.[35] This was
clearly wrong since there were plainly cases where there were responsibilities to act
on the part of others — for example those who depended legitimately on others
because of infancy, disability or unconsciousness.

Expansion of responsibility categories
Indeed the late twentieth century saw a major expansion in the categories of
persons the courts considered legally responsible for third party injuries. Thus,
in addition to direct duties not to cause injury, the law has recognised duties to
prevent injury:
breach of which may give rise to civil liability. Thus defects in buildings giving
rise to a reasonably foreseeable danger of injury can give rise to liability on
the part of those responsible for building regulation enforcement; prison
officers may be responsible for injuries to those in their custody; railway
authorities responsible for collisions arising out of failure to man crossings; and
electric authorities responsible for injuries associated with failures to maintain
wires and installations; liability in negligence can arise for failure to supervise
mentally incarcerated people, and so on. In each instance legal responsibility
was expanded, not because of any change in our or scientific understandings of
causation, but because of a shift in beliefs about the role law should play. In a
democratic society engaging in self-rule through law, law makers, judges and
others need to be just as conscious of the need to debate the appropriate scope
of legal responsibilities and to see these issues not as ones of fact, evidence,
science or as causation problems, but as what they are — purposes and policy.

### Notlar

[35] Burada kastedilen, common law'da (özellikle İngiliz hukuku) tarihsel olarak benimsenmiş ve "non-feasance" (eylemsizlik; müdahale etmemek) durumlarında üçüncü kişilerin zararlarından yalnızca sınırlı bazı statü sahiplerini (örneğin han sahipleri, hekimler, veliler/vasiler) sorumlu tutan geleneksel kurallardır.

> **⚖️ Türk hukuku karşılaştırması:** Burada tartışılan, özellikle Kanada ve İngiliz common law hukukunda uyarı yükümlülüğü (failure to warn) ve üçüncü kişilerin zararlarını önlemeye yönelik koruyucu yükümlülüklerin kapsamı ile bağlantılı sorumluluk anlayışlarıdır. Türk hukukunda haksız fiil sorumluluğunun çerçevesi TBK m.49 vd. uyarınca çizilir; illiyet bağı bakımından Yargıtay ve doktrinde uygun sebep teorisi (adequate causation) benimsenir. Müdahale eden yeni neden (novus actus interveniens) benzeri durumlarda, mücbir sebep veya zarar görenin/üçüncü kişinin ağır kusuru illiyet bağını kesen haller olarak kabul edilir (TBK m.52). Uyarı yükümlülüğü ve koruma/önleme yükümlülükleri, özellikle hekim sorumluluğu, iş güvenliği, kamu hizmeti kusuru ve gözetim/koruma yükümlülükleri bağlamında, hem özel hukuk (TBK, KTK) hem de kamu hukuku ve idari yargı içtihadında geliştirilmiştir; ancak common law'daki Arndt ve Hollis gibi davalarla bire bir aynı yapıda kodifiye bir düzenleme bulunmamaktadır.
